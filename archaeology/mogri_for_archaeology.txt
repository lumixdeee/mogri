Mogri for Archaeology

A small vocabulary for holding interpretation open when evidence has not yet earned a final distinction.

Canonical basis

Mogri is the irreducible root for a held-open condition in which an important semantic object remains available before a useful distinction is fixed.

In archaeology, the semantic object is usually an interpretation of traces, relations, sequence, use, agency, or meaning.

The physical pit, wall, bone, tool, or deposit is evidence.
Mogri applies to the unresolved interpretation object, not to a hidden substance inside the physical thing.

Local shorthand

Archaeologists can use "unknowns that matter" as shorthand for unresolved evidence-to-interpretation relations that would materially change the reading if resolved.

Unknownness alone is not enough.

A missing fact is Mogri-relevant only when forcing an answer would risk invention, substitution, or loss of a still-live interpretation.

Demogrify

Demogrify means to resolve a specific held distinction to the extent supported by evidence.

Examples:

- residue may demogrify one vessel use
- microwear may demogrify one tool action
- refitting may demogrify sequence
- isotopes may demogrify movement
- dating may demogrify chronology

None of these automatically resolves every social meaning attached to the same object.

Partial demogrification

A result may resolve one part while leaving another part held.

Modified skull fragments may support deliberate body handling while leaving the social role of that handling unresolved.

Mogrification

To mogrify is to place, keep, or restore an interpretation object in Mogri holding.

This may happen because:

- evidence is incomplete
- evidence was lost or moved
- ancient actors concealed a mechanism
- a later label fixed the reading too early
- new evidence reopened a previously settled interpretation

Academic mogrification does not make the past vague.

It restores the interpretation to the weight the evidence can support.

False demogrification

False demogrification occurs when a claim acts as though a held distinction has already been resolved.

Examples:

"This was ritual" can be false demogrification if it ends the question while storage, drainage, reuse, burial, trapping, or later fill remain live.

"This was practical" can be false demogrification if it excludes symbol, memory, status, or death without support.

"No mass grave" does not by itself resolve every question about threat, conflict management, fear, or rare violence.

A field method

Start with the trace.

State what is physically observed.

Name the interpretation object.

Ask which unresolved relation would materially change the reading.

Rank live possibilities by support.

Demogrify only what the evidence resolves.

Leave the remaining live object in Mogri holding.

State what observation or test would resolve the next distinction.

Worked example: stone-lined pit

Known:
edge, fill, depth, relation to floors, finds, and sequence may be partly observed.

Held:
use, original contents, reopening, relation to food, water, bone, body, tool, waste, bait, or later reuse may remain unresolved.

Possible demogrification events:
residue, insect evidence, refitting, micromorphology, dating, or spatial relation.

False demogrification:
calling it "ritual pit" or "storage pit" and then letting that label end the live alternatives before the evidence does.

Gobekli Tepe

Gobekli Tepe is useful because several high-level roles remain live while a large evidence floor already exists.

A Mogri-aware reading does not require all roles to have equal weight.

It can rank ceremonial gathering, feasting, death practice, food work, movement, threat management, storage, route control, and other roles according to current evidence while still holding unresolved relations for later testing.

The point is not to keep the site mysterious.

The point is to prevent one useful label from replacing the larger evidence object.

Writing form

A compact sentence can be:

`The evidence resolves X, leaves Y in Mogri holding, and makes Z a testable possibility rather than a conclusion.`

Why the word helps

Archaeological prose often needs to say:

- this trace is real
- this interpretation is useful
- this part remains unresolved
- this unresolved part still changes the model
- do not invent the missing distinction yet

Mogri gives that handling job a short name.

It does not replace evidence or ordinary archaeological method.
